about

Built for connection through different senses, without relying on your usual patterns.

What it is

A guided Zouk-centered workshop that gradually trains safe blindfold dancing, then moves into a long silent block where the experience becomes deep, minimal, and focused. The main music and partner-dance vocabulary will be Brazilian Zouk.

brazilian zouk connection pattern-breaking sensory awareness close embrace safety-first

Who it’s for

  • Brazilian Zouk dancers from improver+ (below intermediate may find it challenging).
  • The normal participant group is Zoukers. We may invite or accept one or two advanced leaders/followers from other couple dances as rare exceptions, and only with a strong reputation for safety, musicality, and partner care.
  • Solo registrations are welcome (rotation is part of the format).
  • 18+ only.

format

Two parts. Training first. Then the silent blindfold block.

Part 1 — Preparation (≈1.5h)

  • Warm-up, grounding, and sensory orientation
  • Close-embrace safety & boundaries (shown and practiced)
  • Safety without sight: collisions, touch, exploration
  • Gradual accommodation to blindfolds

Part 2 — Silent Block (≈1.5h)

  • Blindfolded, no participant talking (safety exceptions exist)
  • Rotation + moments of anonymity
  • Exploration of new senses and connections
  • Closing debrief & grounding afterwards

safety & consent

Respected boundaries. Practiced signals. Clear rules. Safety overrides everything.

Consent baseline
  • No intimate touch, no surprises, no testing boundaries. Everything stays within socially appropriate close-embrace partner-dance boundaries.
  • No romantic escalation. No kissing, sexual comments, whispering, or “going beyond dancing”.
  • No-go zones are explicitly listed, shown, and repeated before the Silent Block.
Yellow / Red system (practiced)
  • Yellow: reduce intensity / slow down / increase space.
  • Red: full stop. Freeze and give space immediately.
  • No explanation needed. Team actively monitor and can step in.
Touch boundaries (what’s allowed)

Close embrace is part of the format. Within defined limits (shown visually), the workshop may include:

  • Body waves (frame-based and open-position waves)
  • Chest-to-chest contact and kizomba-style hip contact within demonstrated boundaries

Not allowed: no-go zones, breathing in ears, kissing, or any exploratory hand placement outside instructed contact points.

Movement rules (blindfold part)
  • No lifts. No dips. No broad movements.
  • Head movement is minimal and strictly guided. No fast or large-range head motions.
  • Traveling is allowed only when safe for the exercise. The team may stop risky movement immediately.
The Silent Block rule (blindfolds)

Blindfolds stay on in the dance space at all times during the Silent Block.

If you need water, restroom, or support, raise your hand and a Team member will escort you to a private safe area (separate from the dance space). In the safe area, you may remove the blindfold if needed.

Returning to the Silent Block is usually possible, but may be declined if safety or container integrity requires it (e.g., medical concern, repeated distress, not following instructions, disruptive behavior, or safety risk to self/others).

If return is declined, you can rest in a chill area and may be allowed to join the final debrief. No refunds apply in this case.

Signals (silent block)
  • Stop with partner: trained hand squeeze → freeze + give space.
  • Call a team member: raise your hand (team members are trained to notice quickly).
  • Audible exception: if absolutely necessary, a short audible call is allowed to get help. Safety overrides silence.
Hygiene & sobriety
  • Please come sober. No alcohol or recreational drugs before or during the workshop.
  • Perfume discouraged (odorless antiperspirant is fine). Shower recommended.
  • Fresh shirt strongly recommended.
  • No rings/jewelry/watches; short nails recommended.

q&a

All the necessary questions answered.

When and where is it?
Monday 25 May 2026, in the Amsterdam area close to the center. Time window is approximately 17:00–20:00, plus a closing debrief and grounding. The venue is already booked; the exact address will be shared with confirmed participants only, no later than 20 May 2026.
Why is the venue shared only with confirmed participants?
For participant safety and privacy. The confirmed venue is shared only with registered participants.
How long is the workshop and how is it structured?
About 3 hours total, split into two ~1.5-hour sections:
  1. Preparation & training (signals, boundaries, safe blindfold dancing)
  2. The ~1.5-hour Silent Blindfold Block (no participant talking; safety exceptions exist), followed by a debrief/grounding.
Is there a break?
Yes, one main break between the two sections. The first part is also more flexible for quick step-outs if needed.
Who is this workshop for?
Primarily for Brazilian Zouk dancers (improver+). The main dance and music will be Zouk, and it can be challenging for dancers below intermediate. The normal participant group is Zoukers. We may invite or accept one or two advanced leaders/followers from other couple dances as rare exceptions, and only with a strong reputation for safety, musicality, and partner care.
Can I join if I’ve never danced blindfolded?
Yes. The first part includes gradual accommodation and safety training. If you have a known strong fear of darkness or blindfolding, this workshop may not be suitable.
Is it okay if I’m anxious about blindfolding?
Many people are. Safety measures, clear rules, and instructed team are present to support you. If unexpected anxiety arises during the workshop, the team will help.
Is it okay if I know in advance I can’t stay blindfolded for the full silent block?
If you already know you won’t be able to stay in the Silent Block for the full duration, it’s recommended not to join.
Is registration open?
Yes. The form is public, but the workshop is Zouk-centered and confirmations are curated.
What is the registration flow?
Apply → verify your email → background review → if confirmed, receive a confirmation email and payment link. Applications are reviewed for Zouk fit, safety, and role balance.
Do I need to bring ID?
Yes. Please bring a government-issued ID (passport/driver license) that matches the registration name. For container safety, we may ask to check it to confirm the right person is entering.
Can organizers reject an application?
Yes. We will try to maximize openness and inclusivity, but we may still reject an application for safety, balance, or any other reason, with or without explanation.
Is there an age limit?
Yes, 18+ only.
Can I register solo?
Yes. Solo registration is welcome.
Can I register with a partner?
Yes. Add a note about your partner in the registration form; we will try to add you both if spots allow, or tell you otherwise.
Will there be partner rotation?
Yes, rotation is frequent and part of the format, including moments where you may not know who you’re dancing with.
Can I opt out of rotation?
No. Rotation is integral to the workshop design.
Is there any way to avoid dancing with a specific person?
Yes. You can confidentially submit a “do not pair me with X” request for personal reasons, no questions asked. Rotations will be adjusted to satisfy constraints. We still encourage you to try seeing everyone from a fresh perspective in this safe space.
When should I submit a “do not pair” request?
Preferably as soon as the participant list is published, but it can also be submitted at the venue.
Will my “do not pair” request be confidential?
Yes. It is handled confidentially.

Rotations will be adjusted to satisfy the constraints.

Yes. The workshop uses fixed Leader/Follower roles for structure and safety, with strict parity.

No intimate touch, no surprises, no testing boundaries. Everything stays within socially appropriate close-embrace partner-dance boundaries.

Yes. No-go zones will be clearly listed, shown, practiced, and repeated before the Silent Block.

No participant talking during the Silent Block, with safety exceptions only.
Are sensual movements allowed?

Yes, within defined limits demonstrated and reinforced in training.
What are the safety signals during the Silent Block?
